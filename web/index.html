How it works

Three steps, then it gets out of your way

It notices where you work

Billed records which work sites are in front of you and for how long — the domain, the page path, the page title, and two timestamps. The clock stops when you stop.

You name the client once

It groups what it saw by the thing that identifies a client — acme.atlassian.net, github.com/acme-corp — and asks once. That answer becomes a rule and applies forever.

Friday takes ten seconds

Hours per client per day, with ticket keys attached as evidence. Download a CSV, copy invoice lines, or paste a summary into an email. Done.

Privacy

A tool that watches your work had better be honest about it

So here is the whole truth, and you can verify every line of it — Billed ships as readable, unminified source.

What Billed does

  • Stores everything in this browser profile, on this device
  • Keeps a domain, a path, a page title and two timestamps per session
  • Ships with banking, health, adult and personal-social patterns already blocked
  • Lets you block any site, delete any site's history, or wipe everything
  • Exports a full backup as one JSON file, whenever you want

What Billed cannot do

  • Send your activity anywhere — there is no account and no server
  • Read page contents, form fields or keystrokes: it has no content scripts
  • Store query strings, where search terms and tokens live
  • Make any network request at all on a fresh install — it holds no network permission
  • Be seen by your employer or anyone else. It is yours alone

The one exception, stated plainly: if you buy Pro and paste a licence key, Billed asks your permission to contact the licence server, and sends only that key. Decline and the extension keeps working on the free plan.

Details that matter

Built by someone who has argued about an invoice

It never invents time

Merging two blocks of work sums the parts. The gap between them is never billed, even when it is short. Under-report, never over-report.

Idle time is not billable

Walk away and the clock stops. If the browser is killed mid-session, the segment closes at the last confirmed moment — not at whenever it wakes up.

Ticket keys as evidence

PAY-2214, INC0012345, #812 — pulled out of page titles and attached to the line, so a timesheet can survive a question.

Billing increments

6, 10, 15, 30 or 60 minutes, nearest or always up, applied per line the way invoices actually work.

Every rule is inspectable

No model, no guessing. Every hour traces to exactly one rule you wrote, and the most specific rule always wins.

Calls go on by hand

A call or a meeting cannot be detected, and Billed will not invent one. Add it in a few seconds and it sits on the timesheet like any tracked line.

Status-update mode

Salaried too? The same data reformats into a weekly update or performance-review bullets, grouped by project instead of money.

FAQ

The questions people actually ask

Is this spyware?

It is the opposite, and the architecture is the proof rather than the promise. Billed has no account, no server and no content scripts, so it cannot read a page's contents even if it wanted to. A fresh install has no network permission whatsoever. Everything sits in your own browser profile, and there is a button that deletes all of it.

Can my employer or my client see this?

No. Nothing is transmitted, so there is nothing for anyone to look at. Billed is a tool for the person doing the work, not a tool for watching them.

What about the sites I do not want tracked?

Banking, health, adult and personal-social patterns are blocked out of the box. You can add any domain, subdomain or single path to the list, and deleting a site also deletes what was already recorded for it.

Does it work if I am on calls, or off the browser?

Browser time is captured automatically. Time away from the browser is not, by design — Billed would rather show you a true number than quietly invent one. So there is an "Add time" button: pick the client, the date and the hours, write what it was, and it joins the timesheet as a normal line with your description on the invoice. A few seconds per call, and the total is complete as well as honest.

How is this different from Toggl, Harvest or RescueTime?

Those either ask you to start and stop a timer, or give you a productivity report that is not shaped like an invoice. Billed does neither: it reconstructs hours per client with no timer at all, runs entirely on your device, and outputs the thing you actually need on Friday.

What happens to my data if I stop paying?

Nothing is deleted, ever. The free plan simply shows the last seven days again, and everything older is still on your device waiting. You can also download a complete JSON backup at any time, on any plan.

Will it slow my browser down?

It records one short row when you change tabs and once a minute otherwise. There is no injected code on the pages you visit, so the pages themselves are untouched.

I am salaried, not billing anyone. Is it useful?

Yes — switch on status-update mode. The same reconstruction becomes your weekly update to a manager, or the bullet list you wish you had the week before a performance review.
